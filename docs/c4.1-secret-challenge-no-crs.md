# C4.1 successor: secret challenge without a CRS

Status: research ruling only. No Rust, Lean or pod work is authorized by this
document. The seed-only streaming verifier is rejected because its measured
four-thread expansion and fold takes `12.845134539 s`. The immutable C4.1
prover failure, `1.935020839958646x > 1.30x`, remains controlling.

## Decision boundary

This successor excludes common/structured reference strings. It may reuse the
existing verifier-specific base-OT/VOLE/PCG preprocessing and the random-oracle
model already required by Fiat--Shamir. Such private correlated preprocessing
is not a public CRS, but every response-local item remains one-time and must be
burned on abort.

There are two distinct candidates:

1. `C41SC1`, the simple interactive baseline: after the provider fixes the
   registered commitment frontier, the verifier sends one fresh unpredictable
   private challenge packet. Challenge-dependent verifier keys were folded
   offline. The provider then produces the openings, typed fold and closes.
2. `C41NISC1`, the round-free research candidate: during private preprocessing
   the verifier sends a computationally hiding encoding of the same challenge
   packet. After fixing the commitment frontier, the provider evaluates the
   challenge-dependent post-commitment function without learning the packet and
   sends one online message.

`C41NISC1` is preprocess-then-one-message secure computation, not an ordinary
plain-model NIZK and not Fiat--Shamir applied to private verifier coins.

## Fiat--Shamir composition

Fiat--Shamir remains applicable to public-coin challenges outside the private
packet. In `C41SC1`, both parties append the canonical private packet after the
commitment frontier and derive every later public challenge from the transcript
containing it. In `C41NISC1`, the public transcript instead binds the one-time
preprocessing identifier and encoding digest; the secure computation consumes
the associated private packet.

The private packet itself is not replaced by Fiat--Shamir. Its statistical
soundness term must be composed with the random-oracle terms, one-time-state
security and the existing conditional XOR4-MAJ7 assumption. Both total
soundness and weight privacy must remain above 78 bits.

Crucially, the packet must contain every coin that determines the typed query,
not only the final scalar-power bridge challenge `beta`. The current query is

```text
q[cell] = sum_j beta^(j+1) * descriptor_j[cell],
```

and each `descriptor_j` includes GKR/PCS evaluation points derived after prior
prover moves. If those points remain response-derived Fiat--Shamir values, the
verifier cannot precompute the final folded key before seeing the response.

## Why an offline clear challenge is unsound

Sending the packet in clear before the commitment frontier lets a malicious
provider choose its commitments as a function of the challenge. A commitment
to the packet still needs an online opening. A fixed public pool permits
commitment grinding and would need exponential size to retain the current
soundness. A verifier PRF or VRF either leaves the challenge unavailable to the
provider or requires an online evaluation/release. A randomness beacon, VDF or
TEE merely replaces the client round with a new online dependency or trust
assumption.

Without interaction, CRS or trusted hardware, the provider must therefore
compute the challenge-dependent response on a hidden input. Silent NISC gives
this syntax: the receiver masks its input during preprocessing and the sender
later returns the function output in one message. It does not make the function
free.

## C4.1-specific obstruction

The real proof has `640` live bridge corrections, but hiding only their final
`beta` powers is insufficient. The verifier must authenticate

```text
K_q = sum_cell q[cell] * k[cell]
```

against setup-fixed typed keys. The public descriptors are response-dependent,
while the compact seeds expand to `3,110,400` cells. A provider-supplied value
for `K_q` has no setup-fixed reference and would make the MAC check vacuous.

A generic NISC must securely evaluate seed expansion plus the query fold, or
must securely run all post-commitment openings under a packet that also fixes
the descriptor points. Garbled-circuit and generic OT reductions cost at least
linearly in that function. The existing literature provides no construction
that simultaneously establishes:

- a few-KB retained verifier state;
- no response-critical seed expansion on the client;
- less than the current `2,168,980-B` proof headroom;
- no new CRS or trusted hardware;
- no material prover regression; and
- the inherited soundness, weight-ZK, setup and device-memory gates.

## Required analytic screen before implementation

No protocol code is admitted until a design supplies all of the following:

1. the exact commitment frontier and complete private challenge census;
2. an exact factorization of `K_q` showing which work is offline, provider
   online and verifier online;
3. a malicious-secure no-CRS realization from the existing private
   OT/VOLE/PCG setup, including selective-failure and abort leakage;
4. exact online proof bytes, setup traffic, provider state and verifier state;
5. a work count parameterized by the measured bridge sparse-entry census;
6. one-time burn and multi-response security; and
7. a complete soundness/weight-privacy composition above 78 bits.

The prover diagnostics already expose bridge count and sparse entries. Those
counters are the first empirical input to a specialized NISC screen; they do
not authorize a pod run.

Absent such a factorization, `C41SC1` is the only practical candidate: it pays
one verifier-to-provider message after commitment, retains pre-folded verifier
keys, and keeps Fiat--Shamir for the remaining public challenges.

## Literature ruling

- Boyle et al., *Efficient Two-Round OT Extension and Silent Non-Interactive
  Secure Computation*, [ePrint 2019/1159](https://eprint.iacr.org/2019/1159),
  gives no-trusted-setup silent preprocessing from base OT/PCG and the required
  masked-receiver-input syntax. General functions still reduce to garbled
  circuits; correlations are not free or reusable without a security proof.
- Ishai et al., *Efficient Non-Interactive Secure Computation*,
  [EUROCRYPT 2011](https://www.iacr.org/archive/eurocrypt2011/66320411/66320411.pdf),
  establishes general NISC from OT but explicitly pays per circuit gate and
  discusses bounded reuse/selective-failure issues.
- Mohassel--Rosulek, *Non-Interactive Secure 2PC in the Offline/Online and
  Batch Settings*, [ePrint 2017/125](https://eprint.iacr.org/2017/125), moves
  circuit-dependent work offline but retains garbled-circuit-scale setup.
- Couteau--Zarezadeh, *Non-Interactive Secure Computation of Inner-Product from
  LPN and LWE*, [ePrint 2023/072](https://eprint.iacr.org/2023/072), has linear
  encodings and defines its direct NIIP using a CRS; it is excluded here.
- Baum et al., *Mac'n'Cheese*,
  [ePrint 2020/1410](https://eprint.iacr.org/2020/1410), confirms that
  Fiat--Shamir can coexist with VOLE preprocessing for public-coin rounds. It
  does not turn a verifier-private challenge into a public Fiat--Shamir coin.
- Goldreich--Oren, *Definitions and Properties of Zero-Knowledge Proof
  Systems*, [Journal of Cryptology 7 (1994)](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF00195207),
  rules out treating a nontrivial plain-model one-message ZK proof as a free
  substitute for setup or interaction.
