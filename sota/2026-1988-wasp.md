Abstract Zero-knowledge proofs (ZKPs) based on vector oblivious linear evaluation (VOLE) excel in prover efficiency but typically require linear communication and verification. Antman (Weng et al., CCS ’22) introduced information-theoretic polynomial authentication codes (IT-PACs) to achieve sublinear communication: *𝑂*(*𝐵*+*𝐶*) for SIMD (single-instruction-multiple-data) circuits and *𝑂*(*𝐵³* +*𝐶*) for general circuits, where *𝑁*=*𝐵*·*𝐶* is the total circuit size and *𝐵,𝐶* are batch size and subcircuit size, respectively. Antman++ (Bui et al., J. Cryptol. ’25) further reduced the general-case communica- tion to *𝑂*(*𝐵*+*𝐶*). However, these protocols remain interactive and cannot be made non-interactive via traditional techniques like Fiat- Shamir, due to limited functionalities of IT-PACs; also, the verifier of Antman++ is not succinct for processing *𝑁*×*𝑁* public matrices. In this work, we present a succinct non-interactive ZKP system Wasp. Specifically, (1) we enhance the IT-PAC primitive to a fully functional polynomial commitment scheme (PCS) with the support of generic evaluation openings. With this PCS, we construct Wasp S, a non-interactive ZKP for SIMD circuits based on Antman; also, we build Wasp G, a general zkSNARK with constant verifier time and proof size based on the Plonkish constraint system. (2) We optimize the SIMD-to-general compiler from Antman++ by exploit- ing sparse representation of matrices and extending preprocessing techniques to the SIMD setting, and achieve a sublinear verifier. All our protocols achieve non-interactivity in the VOLE-hybrid model (i.e., given preprocessed VOLE correlations). Experiments show the non-interactive verifier of our SIMD zkSNARK Wasp S is1 ∼ 2 orders of magnitude faster than the interactive one of Antman; when compiled with our compiler, the general-case verifier is2 ∼ 3 orders of magnitude faster than the one in Antman++. 1 Introduction Zero-knowledge proofs (ZKPs) enable a party to show the truth of an NP statement without revealing any information other than the truth of the statement itself. Recent decades have witnessed great advancements in ZKPs since they were first introduced by Goldwasser et al. in 1985 [22]. Among diverse types of ZKPs, zero- knowledge succinct¹ non-interactive arguments of knowledge (zk- SNARKs) stand out due to their sublinear proof sizes and verifier time, and are especially prevalent in real-world deployments (such as Plonk [21] and Marlin [15]). Vector oblivious linear evaluation (VOLE)-based ZK protocols [6, 10, 18, 40, 41, 43] are an emerging class of ZKPs that feature high prover efficiency. They are typically built on the learning parity with noise (LPN) assumption, and are a type of designated-verifier non-interactive ZK (DV-NIZK) protocol given VOLE correlations generated by a preprocessing phase. They adopt a gate-by-gate prov- ing paradigm, and their provers can be much more efficient than

However, they typically entail linear proof size and verification time. Antman [42] first achieves sublinear communication cost among VOLE-based ZKPs, for both SIMD (single instruction multiple data) circuits and generic circuits. For a SIMD circuit consisting of *𝐵* instances of a subcircuit of size *𝐶* (and thus the total circuit size is *𝑁*=*𝐵*·*𝐶*), it is natural to view their wire values as a matrix: each column describes one execution, while each row contains the *𝐵* values carried by the same wire across all executions. Instead of authenticating these *𝐵* values separately, Antman encodes each row as a polynomial and authenticates it with a new notion called information-theoretic polynomial authentication code (IT-PAC). This yields *𝑂*(*𝐵*+*𝐶*) communication, minimized to *𝑂*(*𝑁* 1/2 ) when *𝐵*=*𝐶*=*𝑁* 1/2. To handle a general circuit, Antman reorganizes the general circuit into SIMD subcircuits with padded dummy gates, which are then proved using its SIMD ZKP. Note that correct SIMD executions alone do not guarantee a valid general-circuit execution; it is also necessary to ensure the consistency of wire connections among these SIMD subcircuits w.r.t. the original circuit. Antman thus separately performs a wire consistency check, and this addi- tionally incurs*𝑂*(*𝐵³*)communication cost, resulting in*𝑂*(*𝐵³* +*𝐶*) total communication, or*𝑂*(*𝑁* 3/4 ) with the optimal choice *𝐵*=*𝑁* 1/4. Antman is a pioneering work in the VOLE-based ZKP family for achieving sublinear communication cost. However, we notice that it cannot be made non-interactive as previous VOLE-based ZKPs using folklore techniques (e.g., the Fiat-Shamir transforma- tion [19]). This arises from its IT-PAC mechanism. At a high level, an IT-PAC is a designated-verifier commitment to a polynomial *𝑓* : the prover holds a secret *𝑓*, and authenticates it via a hidden evaluation of *𝑓* at a secret point Λ, i.e., *𝑓*(Λ), using the additively homomorphic encryption (AHE) and VOLE primitives. Informally, binding holds as the prover does not know Λ, and hiding holds as *𝑓*(Λ) is masked when authenticating it using VOLE. However, unlike a fully functional polynomial commitment scheme (PCS), an IT-PAC lacks the capability to open the committed polynomial at any fresh verifier-chosen point. Antman’s multiplication check therefore requires the verifier to reveal Λ after the polynomials have been authenticated to enable the prover evaluating them at Λ, and then complete the check. This online message cannot be removed by a direct Fiat-Shamir transformation [19], since Fiat-Shamir can only derive a fresh public challenge from the transcript, whereas Λ must already be fixed and kept secret when the IT-PACs are gener- ated. Once revealed, Λ must also be refreshed before authenticating another batch of computations, and additional consistency checks between the old and refreshed IT-PACs may be required. A follow-up work [12] proposed a compiler from SIMD to gen- eral ZKP, and built Antman++ by applying the compiler to the SIMD ZKP of Antman. It revisits the wire consistency check prob- lem in Antman. To ensure the witnesses are consistent with the wire connections specified by the circuit, it collects all wire values in a global vector and uses three public sparse matrices (that de- scribe the circuit structure) to select the left input, right input, and output values of all multiplication gates. Wire consistency is then re- duced to three matrix-vector product equalities. It then compresses

A New PCS from VOLE.We extend IT-PAC to a fully functional PCS, with the support of opening the committed polynomials at arbitrary points. The core technique is utilizing the polynomial divisibility property (as in the KZG10 PCS [28]) to create a witness polynomial; by committing the witness polynomial with an IT- PAC, the correctness of evaluations can be checked with VOLE techniques. It enables modular design of ZKPs by combining a PIOP (polynomial interactive oracle proof) system with a PCS, and may be of independent interest. The complexities and properties of our PCS greatly resemble KZG10, i.e., the evaluation proof has *𝑂*(1) size and verification time, and is friendly for batching. Besides, as we use VOLE and AHE as the underlying cryptographic tools rather than the more costly pairing and group operations as in KZG10, our PCS is more efficient in committing and proving. Based on this, we construct Wasp S andWasp G. •Wasp S targets SIMD circuits, built upon the SIMD ZKP from Antman. By adapting the multiplication check of Antman to a PIOP and combining it with our PCS, we can achieve a non- interactive verifier with higher concrete efficiency. •Wasp G is a zkSNARK for general circuits, constructed by com- bining our new PCS with the PIOP system from Plonk [21]. Consequently, it achieves constant proof size and verification time³, which, to the best of our knowledge, is the first VOLE- based ZKP achieving this property.

1.2 Related Works VOLE-based ZKPs.This line of work was initiated by Boyle et al. [10], who introduced the first VOLE-based ZKP based on the LPN assumption. Subsequent works significantly improved upon this foundation. Wolverine [40], LPZK [18], LPZKv2 [17], Quicksil- ver [43], and Mac’n’Cheese [6] employ the authenticated multiplica- tion triples to prove the multiplication gates for both arithmetic and boolean circuits. The SOTA work JesseQ [33] further reduces the online prover cost, with a relatively heavy preprocessing phase. All

these protocols feature linear prover complexity and are highly effi- cient, but they incur linear proof size and verification time. Antman is the first VOLE-based ZKP with sublinear communication cost, but lacks the non-interaction property; while Antman++ further improves the communication cost, its verifier time is linear. Lin et al. [31] also achieved sublinear costs; however, its higher round complexity *𝑂*(*𝑑*log*𝑁*) would incur more efficiency loss when ap- plying the Fiat-Shamir transformation over small fields, consider- ing round-by-round soundness. The VOLE-in-the-Head (VOLE-itH) technique [5] is an orthogonal line that compiles a broad class of designated-verifier VOLE-based ZKPs into publicly verifiable ones. It enables the prover to first commit to the randomness underly- ing the VOLE correlations before receiving a public challenge, and subsequently opens all but one branch to allow any verifier to re- construct and check the verifier-side correlations. Applying the Fiat–Shamir transformation then yields publicly verifiable proofs in the random-oracle model. This approach largely preserves the lightweight linear prover cost of VOLE-based ZKP. However, it cannot provide succinctness if the underlying VOLE-based ZKP is not succinct, and it typically retains the linear proof size and verification time of VOLE-based ZKPs. Our work continues the VOLE-based line of research, and can achieve non-interaction, constant proof size and verification time for the first time in the VOLE-based ZKP family. Moreover, our pro- tocols are compatible with the VOLE-itH technique. It can help to remove the preprocessing phase for generating VOLE correlations. 4

Lattice-based zkSNARKs.Boneh et al. [27] proposed a general template for building lattice-based SNARKs by combining a lin- ear PCP (probabilistically checkable proof) [24] with a linear-only encryption scheme. Given this, Ishai et al. [27] proposed a DV- zkSNARK with constant proof size and verifier time. However, due to the reliance on linear PCP, it requires an expensive circuit- dependent setup to generate a long CRS (e.g., it takes 37 minutes to generate a CRS of size 5.3 GB for a circuit of size2 20 ). Luna [38] improved upon this construction with a shorter proof size, using a new candidate linear-only homomorphic encryption scheme called Module Half-GSW. However, the CRS size remains large. Another line of research focuses on lattice-based PCS [1, 26, 34, 39]. Wee et al. [39] introduced a base folding technique for vector commitments, extended to polynomial commitments via Lagrange interpolation, achieving logarithmic proof size and verification time under SIS. However, the construction relies on relaxed soundness. SLAP [1] addressed this limitation through a refined polynomial decomposition that achieves standard soundness under Module-SIS. Greyhound [34] focuses on prover efficiency through FFT-based lattice arithmetic and reduces prover complexity to *𝑂*(*𝑁*log*𝑁*). However, the proof size remains*𝑂*(log*𝑁*)rather than constant.

6.1 Implementation We instantiate the AHE primitive using the BGV scheme [11], and implement our PCS and the Wasp system in C++. Inner-product Computation.RLWE-based AHE schemes sup- port SIMD-style plaintext packing, allowing multiple messages to be processed in parallel within a single ciphertext. However, the inner- product computations in our PCS involve cross-slot operations that require expensive ciphertext rotations. G Optimizations of Instantiating AHE Following previous works, we use Ring-LWE (RLWE)–based schemes (such as BGV [11]) to instantiate the AHE primitive and construct our PCS. Below we present some practical optimizations for our instantiation.

G.1 Inner-product Computation RLWE-based AHE schemes support SIMD-style plaintext packing, which allows multiple independent messages to be processed in parallel within a single ciphertext. Concretely, the plaintext space is decomposed into multiple independent slots, enabling homomor- phic operations to be applied slot-wise with the cost of a single ciphertext operation. However, in our PCS, the prover’s commitment to polynomials involves inner-product computations that aggregate values across slots within a ciphertext. Such cross-slot operations are expensive, as they require a sequence of ciphertext rotations and additions, resulting in significant computational overhead during the online phase. To address this inefficiency, we adopt two alternative meth- ods to reduce the prover’s online computation cost.
